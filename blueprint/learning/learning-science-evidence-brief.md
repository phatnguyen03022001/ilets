## 1. Learning Philosophy — `[CONSENSUS]`
- **Deliberate practice** (Ericsson et al. 1993): expertise grows through focused, goal-oriented practice with feedback — not mere experience or volume. The "10,000-hour rule" is a popular overclaim; practice explains meaningful but limited variance (Macnamara et al. 2016: ~4% in education).
- **Skill Acquisition Theory** for adult L2 (DeKeyser 2001): declarative → procedural → automatic; adults proceduralize more slowly than children.
- **Output Hypothesis** (Swain 2005): production (noticing, hypothesis-testing, reflection) drives language learning. `[CONTESTED]` input vs. output → modern view: **both** required (input builds base, output consolidates).
- *IELTS relevance:* productive skills (Writing/Speaking) need structured output practice; automatization is the central adult-L2 challenge.
- *Caution:* over-emphasizing practice quantity over quality; plateau effects in adults.

## 2. Mastery Model — `[CONSENSUS]`
- **Mastery learning** (Bloom 1968; Guskey 2010): most learners reach high competence given sufficient time + appropriate instruction; advance on a criterion (~80–90%), not a curve.
- **Criterion- over norm-referenced** for skill development; IELTS band descriptors are criterion-referenced standards.
- Meta-analysis: moderate positive effect (Kulik et al. 1990).
- *IELTS relevance:* Band 3–9 maps naturally to criterion-referenced mastery; band transitions are non-linear (4→5 ≠ 7→8).
- *Caution:* mastery needs individualized time, which can conflict with fixed test dates; norming samples should inform, not define, mastery.

## 3. Practice Model — `[CONSENSUS]`
- **Retrieval practice / testing effect** (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Adesope et al. 2017): retrieving > restudying for retention; moderate, robust effects.
- **Spacing / distributed practice** (Cepeda et al. 2008): spaced > massed; optimal gap ~10–20% of the retention interval.
- **Interleaving** (Dunlosky et al. 2013): mixed > blocked practice for transfer. *(Dunlosky rated classroom utility cautiously; later work is more positive.)*
- **Worked examples / scaffolding** (Sweller et al. 2019; Barbieri et al. 2023, math): reduce load for novices; **expertise-reversal** — fade scaffolding as skill grows.
- *IELTS relevance:* spaced retrieval of vocab/grammar; interleave task types; worked examples for Writing Task 2 → fade to independent practice; reduce scaffolding for Band 7+.
- *Caution:* over-scaffolding advanced learners; domain effects smaller for language than motor skills.

## 4. Assessment Philosophy — `[CONSENSUS]`
- **Formative > summative** for raising achievement (Black & Wiliam 1998; Sortwell 2024, K-12): ongoing assessment **for** learning, moderate-to-large effects.
- **Feedback** (Hattie & Timperley 2007): three questions (feed-up / feed-back / feed-forward) and four levels (task / process / self-regulation / self). Task-level feedback suits novices; self-level (praise) is weakest. Large effect (~d 0.7).
- **Feedback timing** `[CONTESTED]`: immediate helps novice/conceptual acquisition; delayed can aid retention/transfer by forcing effortful retrieval. Optimal timing is task- and stage-dependent.
- *IELTS relevance:* mock tests + detailed criterion-referenced feedback; task-level feedback for Band 3–5, process-level for 6–7.
- *Caution:* feedback overload reduces learning; praise alone is ineffective; much ed-research evidence quality is low-to-moderate.

## 5. Progression Model — `[CONTESTED]`
- **Mastery-gated:** advance only when a criterion is met (Bloom 1968; Kulik et al. 1990) — strongest fit for skill domains.
- **Adaptive:** personalized path; effective when theory-driven (VanLehn 2011; AI-in-L2 meta-analyses), variable otherwise.
- **Linear:** weakest fit for adult skill development.
- *IELTS relevance:* mastery-gated **across** band thresholds; adaptive sequencing **within** a band.
- *Caution:* over-gating demotivates; adaptive systems need transparency; robust prerequisite mapping required.

## 6. Prerequisite Model — `[OPEN]`
- **Direct evidence on optimal L2 prerequisite structures is limited.** Indirect support: cognitive-load element interactivity (Sweller et al. 2019); hierarchical progressions validated mainly in mathematics, not language.
- *IELTS relevance:* vocabulary/grammar plausibly foundational to all skills; reading→writing (argument structure), listening→speaking (pronunciation/intonation) are reasonable inferences, **not** empirically established prerequisites.
- *Caution:* over-specifying prerequisites creates bottlenecks; adults enter with heterogeneous knowledge; some skills develop synergistically (circular dependencies possible).

## 7. Review Philosophy — `[CONSENSUS]`
- **Forgetting curve** (Ebbinghaus 1885; Murre & Dros 2015): rapid initial loss; **expanding retrieval intervals** (~1→3→7→14→30 days) restore retention.
- **Spacing + retrieval** jointly beat massed restudy for consolidation/relearning.
- *IELTS relevance:* expanding-interval review of vocabulary/grammar; mix immediate and delayed review of task strategies.
- *Caution:* intervals too long or too short both reduce effectiveness; forgetting rates vary by learner and material.

## 8. AI's Role in Learning — `[CONTESTED] / emerging`
- **ITS effectiveness** (VanLehn 2011): ITS ≈ d 0.76, human tutoring ≈ d 0.79, both ≫ classroom (~d 0.25). ITS **not definitively superior** to human tutoring.
- **AI in L2** (Lee & Lee 2024; Wu et al. 2024; Saarela et al. 2026): consistent **large positive effects** for language learning; effectiveness varies by application and algorithm quality.
- **Limits/risks:** less nuanced than humans on complex productive feedback; lower motivation; over-reliance erodes metacognition; hallucination risk in AI-generated content; bias and privacy concerns.
- *IELTS relevance:* strong for vocab/grammar drills and objective scoring (Reading/Listening); moderate for Writing; weaker for Speaking. Best use: AI for high-volume practice + objective scoring; human (or human-verified) feedback for complex productive tasks.
- *Caution:* AI-only is insufficient for high-stakes prep; AI-generated content needs verification.

## 9. Content Hierarchy — `[OPEN]`
- **Foundational → advanced** progression supported broadly (Bloom's taxonomy; learning-progressions research).
- **Grain size** `[OPEN]`: CLT suggests smaller grain for novices, larger for advanced (expertise reversal); direct L2 evidence limited.
- **Micro-skill vs. whole-task** `[OPEN]`: mixed evidence; component practice then integration is defensible for novices.
- *IELTS relevance:* foundational layers (vocab, grammar, pronunciation) → task-specific strategies; decompose complex tasks for lower bands, integrate for higher.
- *Caution:* over-fragmentation impedes integration; premature integration yields fragile learning; isolated skills may not transfer.

## Decision Forks — evidence informs but does not decide

These are genuine product/philosophy choices (Founder-owned). Evidence-based recommendation in italics; the choice remains open.

1. **Progression model** — strict vs. lenient vs. hybrid mastery-gating; how many gates per band. *Rec: mastery-gated across band thresholds; adaptive within a band.*
2. **AI vs. human feedback** — AI-only vs. hybrid vs. human-only; which tasks get human feedback. *Rec: hybrid — AI for high-volume practice + objective scoring; human/human-verified for Writing & Speaking.*
3. **Content grain size** — fine micro-skills vs. coarse vs. adaptive. *Rec: adaptive by band (fine for low bands, coarser for high).*
4. **Review schedule** — fixed expanding intervals vs. adaptive vs. performance-graded (review at ~80–90% accuracy). *Rec: performance-graded spacing.*
5. **Formative/summative balance** — 90/10 vs. 70/30 vs. 50/50. *Rec: ~70/30 (formative-dominant).*
6. **Interleaving strategy** — full vs. moderate vs. blocked-with-review. *Rec: moderate (within skill; review across skills).*
7. **Scaffolding approach** — heavy vs. gradual-fading vs. minimal. *Rec: gradual fading by expertise.*

### Principle ↔ evidence conflicts to surface (per Founder meta-rule)
- **Principle #7 "Immediate feedback"** vs. evidence that delayed feedback aids retention/transfer → propose stage-dependent timing (immediate for novices/acquisition; delayed where retention/transfer is the goal).
- **Principle #4 "Strong prerequisite enforcement"** vs. `[OPEN]`/limited evidence and bottleneck risk → propose enforced *core* prerequisites with flexibility elsewhere.
- **Principle #5 "Deliberate practice"** vs. overclaim risk → keep, but explicitly reject the "hours volume" framing; emphasize quality + feedback.
- **Principle #10 "Minimize unnecessary cognitive load"** vs. "desirable difficulties" → keep the "unnecessary" qualifier; preserve intentional productive load (retrieval, interleaving).
