# Assessment Philosophy
*Element 6. Implements [philosophy.md](philosophy.md) #6 (frequent formative), #7, and decisions [LD-002](decisions.md), [LD-003](decisions.md).*

## Purpose
Define the assessment philosophy and feedback model. (Instrument design lives in `../assessment/`.)

## Evidence ([brief §4](learning-science-evidence-brief.md))
- **Formative > summative** for raising achievement (Black & Wiliam 1998; Sortwell 2024, K-12).
- **Criterion-referenced** (not norm-referenced) for skill mastery.
- **Feedback** (Hattie & Timperley 2007): feed-up / feed-back / feed-forward; levels task → process → self-regulation → self. Task-level suits novices; self-level (praise) weakest.

## Model
- **Formative-dominant, criterion-referenced, continuous.** **Decided (~70/30, Founder-approved 2026-07-16):** ~70% formative / 30% summative. Summative includes band-approximating mock tests and **Exam-Preparation** instruments ([LD-005](decisions.md)) — these diagnose readiness but do **not** certify mastery.
- **Feedback timing** ([LD-003](decisions.md)): stage-dependent —
  - Acquisition (Band 3–5): immediate, specific, actionable.
  - Consolidation / transfer (Band 6–9): increasingly retrieval-first / delayed.
- **Feedback level by stage:** task-level for novices → process / self-regulation for advanced.
- **Feedback provider** ([LD-002](decisions.md)): AI-primary, all skills; calibrated confidence for Writing/Speaking; human optional.

## Cautions
- Feedback overload reduces learning; praise alone is ineffective; evidence quality in much ed-research is low-to-moderate.
